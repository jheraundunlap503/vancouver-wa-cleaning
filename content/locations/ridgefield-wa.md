## Why Ridgefield Needs a Different Kind of First Clean

Ridgefield is the fastest growing city in Washington State, [growing at 6.81 percent a year](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/ridgefieldcitywashington), and almost all of that growth is master-planned subdivisions under continuous construction rather than existing homes changing hands. That changes what most cleaning requests in Ridgefield actually are. They are not a maintenance visit on a lived-in house, they are the first clean a brand new house has ever had, and a crew that only knows how to maintain an occupied home is not scoped for that job.

The work is driven by how many homes close in a given month, not by a steady maintenance calendar. A subdivision finishing its third phase can mean a dozen new homes needing the same first clean within a few weeks of each other.

## What Move-In Cleaning Covers in a New Ridgefield Home

Every new home needs the same list handled before anyone moves a box in.

### Kitchen

Protective film and stickers removed from appliance faces, cabinet interiors wiped of sawdust and packing debris, counters and backsplash, sink and faucet, and the floor cleared of construction grit along the baseboards.

### Bathrooms

Caulk haze and installation residue cleaned off shower glass and mirrors, fixture stickers removed, vanity and counters, toilet inside and out, and tile or vinyl floor cleared of grout haze and dust.

### Living areas and bedrooms

Floor protection film removed and any adhesive residue cleaned off, window tracks cleared of construction debris, baseboards and door frames wiped down, light switch plates and outlet covers, and every sill and ledge dusted.

### Final walkthrough

A pass through every room to catch anything a builder's own crew missed, since this clean often doubles as the last check before the homeowner's own walkthrough.

## The Post Construction Dust Problem

Drywall dust is fine enough to stay airborne and resettle after a single pass, which is why a quick wipe down leaves a home looking clean for a day and dusty again by the weekend. The EPA's guidance on [indoor air quality](https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq) treats fine particulates as an ongoing control problem rather than a one time fix, and that is exactly how a new construction clean has to be approached, with repeat passes and filtered vacuums rather than a single sweep.

Cleaning itself means [removing the soil from a surface](https://www.cleaninginstitute.org/cleaning-levels), and construction dust is soil in the most literal sense. Skipping straight to a disinfectant or an all purpose spray on a surface still coated in drywall dust just moves the dust around instead of removing it.

## Ridgefield Pricing and Scheduling Around New Construction

Pricing in Ridgefield is driven by how much builder residue is left rather than by the size of the house alone. A home handed off clean by the builder's own crew costs less to finish than one still covered in dust and film. Scheduling runs around closing dates and HOA walkthrough windows rather than a fixed weekly calendar, so booking as soon as a closing date is confirmed holds the slot that actually lines up with move-in day.
