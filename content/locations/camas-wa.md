## Caring for Custom Finishes

Natural stone and quartz are not interchangeable when it comes to what is safe to put on them, and most of the damage done to a nice counter comes from a well meaning cleaning product rather than from use. The American Cleaning Institute's basic standard still applies underneath all of it: a surface has to actually be [cleaned, meaning soil and grease removed from it](https://www.cleaninginstitute.org/cleaning-levels), before anything else works on it, and an acidic or abrasive product skips that step while damaging the finish at the same time.

In practice that means a pH neutral cleaner on stone and quartz rather than vinegar or a citrus based spray, a wood specific product on hardwood rather than an all purpose floor cleaner, and microfiber instead of anything abrasive on brushed or matte fixtures. A crew that treats a custom quartz island the same way it treats a laminate counter from decades ago is going to leave marks eventually, even if the counter looks fine the day of the visit.
