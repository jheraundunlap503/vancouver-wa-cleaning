## What a Recurring Visit Covers

Every visit is the same list, which is what makes it possible to do it in a predictable amount of time for a predictable price.

### Kitchen

Counters and backsplash, sink and faucet, exterior of every appliance, stovetop and control panel, microwave interior, cabinet fronts at hand height, table, and floor.

### Bathrooms

Toilet inside and out including the base, shower and tub surround, glass, sink and counter, mirror, fixtures, and floor. Towels straightened, trash out.

### Living areas and bedrooms

Dusting of all reachable surfaces, sills, and visible ledges. Beds made or linens changed if you leave them out. Mirrors and glass. Vacuum on carpet, sweep and mop on hard floors. Entryway given attention out of proportion to its size, because in this climate it needs it.

### Every visit, not just some

Trash and recycling out, high touch surfaces disinfected in the kitchen and bathrooms, and a walk through at the end.

## Choosing a Cadence

| Schedule | Typical price per visit | Suits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weekly | $150 to $210 | Pets, kids, home office, entertaining |
| Every other week | $150 to $260 | Most households |
| Monthly | $200 to $310 | One or two tidy people, out most days |

Weekly costs less per visit than monthly. That surprises people until they think about it. The house never gets far enough gone to need rescuing, so each visit is shorter. Monthly visits cost more because four weeks of accumulation takes real time to undo, every single time.

The American Cleaning Institute's baseline for living spaces is floors [vacuumed or swept and mopped weekly](https://www.cleaninginstitute.org/cleaning-tips/cleaning-schedule), with deep cleaning reserved for a few times a year. Their more useful observation is the one about frequency: the more consistently a house gets cleaned, the less deep cleaning it needs to catch up. That is the entire economic argument for a recurring schedule rather than calling somebody twice a year in a panic.

## Why the First Visit Is Different

The first visit is a deep clean, priced accordingly, and then the rate drops.

The reason is arithmetic. A maintenance visit is scoped for a house that is already in reasonable shape. If the baseboards have two years on them and the shower grout has never been treated, a two hour visit spends all of its time on one bathroom and the rest of the house gets nothing. Doing that work once, properly, is what makes every visit afterward fit in the window and hit the price.

If your house was deep cleaned recently by somebody else, say so when you book. Sometimes the first visit can be priced as a normal one.

## What Gets Disinfected and What Does Not

Cleaning and disinfecting are two different operations, and the order is not optional. Cleaning [removes soil and grease from the surface](https://www.cleaninginstitute.org/cleaning-levels); disinfecting inactivates pathogens on it. A disinfectant sprayed onto a dirty surface is mostly working on the dirt.

On a recurring visit, kitchen and bathroom high touch surfaces get cleaned and then disinfected. Door handles, light switches, faucet handles, the fridge door. Everything else gets cleaned, which is what it needs. A company that tells you it disinfects your entire house every visit is describing something that neither happens nor helps.

## Before You Hire Anyone

Someone on a recurring schedule is in your house every two weeks, often when you are not. That is worth thirty seconds of checking, and this is not a pitch for us specifically.

Washington keeps business registration and workers compensation status on the public record. You can look up any cleaning company operating in this state through the [Labor and Industries verification tool](https://lni.wa.gov/licensing-permits/contractors/hiring-a-contractor/verify-contractor-tradesperson-business) before you hand over a key. Ask for the legal business name. Anyone who gets cagey about giving you one has told you what you needed to know.
