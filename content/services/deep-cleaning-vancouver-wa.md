## What Deep Cleaning Covers in Vancouver, WA

The difference between a standard clean and a deep clean is not effort, it is reach. A standard clean handles the surfaces you look at. A deep clean handles the ones you do not.

### Kitchen

Inside the oven and under the drip pans, inside the refrigerator including the door seals and crisper drawers, the range hood and its filter, cabinet faces and the tops of the upper cabinets, backsplash grout, and the sides of the range and refrigerator where grease collects out of sight.

### Bathrooms

Grout lines in floor and shower tile, the mineral haze on shower glass, the track the door slides in, under and behind the toilet base, inside the vanity, the exhaust fan cover, and the underside of every fixture.

### Living areas and bedrooms

Baseboards along every wall, door frames and door tops, light switch plates, outlet covers, window sills and tracks, blinds slat by slat, ceiling fan blades, light fixtures, and vent covers.

### Under and behind

Furniture two people can move gets moved. Under beds, behind couches, under the dresser. In an occupied house these are the places that never get touched, and they are where a year of dust actually lives.

## Cleaning, Sanitizing, and Disinfecting Are Not the Same Thing

Worth knowing before you compare quotes, because some companies charge for one while describing another.

The American Cleaning Institute draws the line clearly. Cleaning [removes soil, dirt, and grease from a surface](https://www.cleaninginstitute.org/cleaning-levels). Sanitizing reduces germ counts to a level public health codes consider safe. Disinfecting inactivates pathogens outright. They are three different outcomes, and the order matters: a surface has to be cleaned first, because dirt sitting on top of a surface shields whatever is underneath it from a disinfectant.

A deep clean is cleaning, done properly and everywhere. Kitchen and bathroom high touch surfaces get disinfected after that. Nobody needs their bedroom baseboards disinfected, and a company quoting you for it is selling something.

## The Damp Season Problem

This is the part of deep cleaning that matters more here than it does most places, and it is the part most likely to come back three weeks after the crew leaves.

Bathroom mildew, the black speckling along grout lines and the bottom edge of window frames, is a moisture problem wearing a cleaning problem's clothes. The EPA is blunt about it: there is no practical way to eliminate mold spores indoors, so the way to control mold growth is to [control the moisture they need](https://www.epa.gov/mold/what-are-main-ways-control-moisture-your-home). Their targets are relative humidity under 60 percent and ideally between 30 and 50, exhaust fans in bathrooms and kitchens vented to the outside rather than into the attic, and a dryer vented outside.

From November through April in Clark County, hitting those numbers takes deliberate effort. Practically that means running the bathroom fan for a good while after the shower stops rather than switching it off with the light, and cracking a window on the dry days you get.

A deep clean removes what has already grown. Ventilation is what decides whether you are booking another one next winter.

## When People Book It

Three moments account for most deep cleans in Vancouver. Spring, once the rain lets up and the house has been closed since October. Before hosting, when somebody is coming to stay and the guest bathroom suddenly gets looked at properly. And the first visit of a recurring schedule, which is a deep clean by default.

If you are booking for a specific date, give it a week of margin. Deep cleans are longer jobs and there are fewer slots for them in a day than there are for standard cleans.
